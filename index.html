BloodTrace Technical Architecture & Optical Physics

Dual-Wavelength Pre-Sterilization Instrument Screening Prototype

1. Optical Physics & Spectral Specificity

BloodTrace exploits differential absorption and fluorescence spectroscopy between metallic surgical alloys and biological residues:

  • 365 nm UV-A Illumination: Organic cellular proteins and free porphyrins exhibit autofluorescence. Crucially, cleaning detergents with optical brighteners produce bright blue-cyan emission (~430-470 nm), making UV alone prone to false positives.
  • 850 nm NIR Illumination: Passivated 316L/420 surgical stainless steel exhibits ~65% specular reflectance. Deoxygenated and dried hemoglobin (methemoglobin) exhibits strong NIR absorption. Under 850nm, dried blood spots appear as stark dark silhouettes, whereas detergent films remain nearly transparent.
  • Dual-Wavelength Cross-Correlation: Combining UV fluorescence and NIR absorption allows the computer vision pipeline to distinguish true biological residue from detergent films and scratches.

4. Clinical Scope & Regulatory Limitations

Mandatory Regulatory Notice: Optical inspection is intended purely as a rapid, non-destructive pre-screening tool for Sterile Processing Departments (SPD) to catch visible gross residue prior to packaging and autoclaving.

It does not replace validated chemical protein assays (e.g. bicinchoninic acid BCA or ninhydrin swabs) or biological spore indicators. Optical contrast cannot determine microbial sterility or quantify bioburden below the optical resolution limit.
